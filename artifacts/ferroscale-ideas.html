How to read this

00

Three numbers decide everything: effort, impact, and what it is worth if it goes wrong. Impact is judged against one question — does this make the app more trusted, faster, or more valuable to a working metal shop? — because those three are the only currencies a shop cares about. A feature that is technically elegant and answers none of them is marked low impact, however good the code would be.

Effort

S hours · M days · L a week or more, including tests, e2e, and the changelog entry that AGENTS.md requires for every user-visible change.

Impact

5 = the app is a different product afterwards. 3 = a real shop would notice and use it. 1 = tidy, and honestly optional. Impact assumes the feature is built well; a half-built 5 is a 2.

Status

extends shipped means the foundation already exists and the idea is the next step on it — usually worth less effort than the tag suggests, because the hard part was the earlier release.

Lands in

The file a developer would open first. It is there so the list is not wishful thinking — every idea names a real home in the codebase, and the ones that need a new file say so.

Big bets

—

The list above is a hundred incremental moves inside the current product. These six are different bets: each one changes what the app is, and each one is written with what it costs and where it breaks. A bet is worth doing only if the "why" survives the answer to "and what stops being true if we do this?"

FerroScale Quote

Lvalue: document
The bet
The calculator becomes a quoting product: every project ends in a real document — branded PDF, line items, terms, margin, validity — that goes to a customer and comes back signed. The command bar stays, but it becomes the input to a document rather than the product itself.
Why now
Every feature already shipped (projects, BOM, margins, paint coats, labour hours, procurement, printable quote) points at the same missing object: there is no quote. The value of the app today stops at a number; the value a shop wants is a price a customer accepts. The groundwork — BOM, margin, customer records, price book — is already built and idle without this.
First step
A real PDF route for one project: company header from Settings, line items from the BOM, VAT and margin, terms, and a QR back to the live calculation. Client-side print stylesheet first (zero deps, works offline), library only if the output demands it.
What it breaks
Print layout becomes a permanent surface that needs its own e2e coverage, and "no account, no tracking" starts to strain once a customer's name and price live on a link. Decide early whether a quote link is a public URL with the customer's data in it.

FerroScale Shop

Lvalue: money saved
The bet
Extend the product from estimating what a job needs to knowing what the shop already owns: a stock and offcut register, with remnant lengths recorded at every cut, and the optimiser and the estimator both reading it. Buying steel is often the largest single line on a job.
Why now
The 1D bar optimiser already exists and already answers "how many 6 m bars do I need" — but it assumes the shop buys everything new. A shop with 2.4 m of HEA 120 in the rack is either free material or scrap. Nothing in the app models inventory, so the optimiser quietly recommends buying steel the shop already owns.
First step
An offcut ledger: after a project, every cut becomes a remnant (profile, length, location). The optimiser then subtracts remnants from the buy list and reports "1 bar needed, 2 remnants cover it". Small, contained, and immediately money.
What it breaks
Inventory is state that goes stale and must be maintained by hand — the opposite of the app's current promise that you never maintain anything. Every design decision has to survive "will a shop keep this up to date?"

FerroScale Field

Lvalue: capture
The bet
A capture-only mode for the person on site: measure, photograph, type a rough note, and queue it offline. It syncs later into the estimator's line, where the arithmetic and the standards live.
Why now
The estimator and the measurer are different people in most shops — one with a laptop and a price book, one with a tape measure and a phone. Today the second person has to call the first, who then types it in. The PWA is already installable and offline-capable; the missing piece is a mode that captures without calculating.
First step
A capture queue in the existing local storage: each entry is a free-text line plus optional photo and location, stored with a timestamp, presented as "3 measurements waiting" on the estimator's desk.
What it breaks
Photos mean blob storage in the sync layer, which was explicitly rejected in 3.19.0 because base64 blobs ride on every Drive push. Queueing text-only measurements is the version that survives that decision.

FerroScale Public Data

Mvalue: reach
The bet
Publish the dataset and the engine as a public product: metal-core on npm, a documented JSON/CSV API over the size tables, and server-rendered pages per size that work without JavaScript. The calculator stays free; the data becomes the asset.
Why now
AGENTS.md already insists metal-core stay UI-free and i18n-free so other surfaces can reuse it — this bet is that promise taken literally. A competitor's engineers can build on the same catalogue, the shop's SEO is solved by the same tables, and the app's authority comes from being the source rather than a consumer of it.
First step
One size page done properly: dimensions, kg/m, section properties with their source citations, and a form that calculates server-side. One page, done to the standard of a reference document, is a better test than a hundred thin ones.
What it breaks
Public data means public error reports. The 3.26.0 release — five wrong channel areas and a tautological benchmark — is exactly the kind of story that must never happen on a public surface. The coverage test has to be mandatory for publication, not optional.

FerroScale White Label

Lvalue: revenue
The bet
Let a steel merchant or fabricator embed a branded calculator on their own site — their colours, their price list, their contact details — with the same engine underneath. The app becomes infrastructure.
Why now
Every merchant's site already has a quote form that emails a human. A merchant who embeds a calculator with their own stock prices converts better and stops fielding "how much is 12 m of HEA 140" emails — which is a lead, sold at a 0% margin, to a competitor's price list.
First step
An iframe-able mini bar with a `?q=`-style API and a per-tenant price list, plus a link back. The share-link and price-book work already exists; this is mostly packaging and a tenant key.
What it breaks
"No account, no tracking" is a real promise to the current user. Tenants, branding and rate limits all imply a server, a key, and someone who owns the uptime. This is the bet most likely to be wrong about the audience.

FerroScale Advisor

Lvalue: reach
The bet
An advisor layer over the local catalogue: you describe the job in a sentence, it proposes a spec and a price, and every number it states is computed locally by the same engine you already trust. The model never computes — it only translates intent into a query the parser understands.
Why now
The grammar is strong but the *entry point* is still specialist. `hea120 6m x2 s235` requires knowing that HEA is the right family, that a beam is 6 m, and that S235 is the default. A sentence in the shop's own vocabulary ("six metre 120 beams, two of them, mild steel") should be enough — and the command bar is already the natural place to type it.
First step
Not the model. First, the vocabulary layer: local shop names for profiles, synonyms per locale, and a dialect of the query language — then measure how often people still fail to type a valid query. If the offline grammar closes 90% of the gap, the model earns its place only for the rest.
What it breaks
Trust. An LLM in the bar that produces a confident, plausible and wrong answer is the single worst thing this product could do — the whole thesis is that the number is traceable. The translation must always be shown as a confirmable query, never applied silently, and the offline path must be complete.

Suggested waves

—

Not a schedule — an order. The waves below are arranged so that each one makes the next one cheaper: foundations before features, trust before reach, the bar before the documents it fills in.

Wave 1 · Foundations — make the next wave safe

  • 84 Shell decomposition — the shell and desktop command components are 1.3–2.3k lines; every idea in the bar section is safer once the query state, keyboard handling and toast state are hooks.
  • 85 Component tests for command interactions — chip editing, keyboard routing, ghost accept, history arrows. The precondition for touching the bar without fear.
  • 1 Per-line note token — the smallest grammar extension, and the one that unlocks the most downstream work.
  • 60 Automatic local backup snapshots — the cheapest insurance in the catalogue, and the only one that protects against silent data loss.

Wave 2 · The bar learns

  • 15 Shop profile defaults · 17 Learning from corrections · 16 Cross-device habits · 20 Repeat-with-one-change · 11 Query diff
  • The theme: the bar stops asking questions it already knows the answer to. Every item here removes keystrokes from a query the shop types twenty times a week.

Wave 3 · The answer earns its trust

  • 70 Clickable citations · 71 Explicit certainty · 24 Real EN tolerance classes · 23 Standard-length snapping
  • The theme: the app's differentiator against every other calculator is traceable numbers. These make that visible instead of merely true.

Wave 4 · Fabricator's tool

  • 21 Cut-length pricing · 22 Cost breakdown waterfall · 25 Coating weight · 27 Rebar & threaded rod · 30 Supplier surcharge rules · 106 Offcut ledger
  • The theme: the app stops being a calculator and starts pricing the work around the steel.

Wave 5 · The document
